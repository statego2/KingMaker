# Chapter 5 — THE PRICE OF A MINISTRY

Purpose:
- show that formal portfolio prestige and actual implementation power are different,
- introduce Silas as a strategist who values procedural leverage,
- turn coalition principles into explicit commitments.

Key systems:
- implementation chokepoints,
- agenda control,
- neutral-rule counterplay,
- coalition contract architecture,

Silas's committee-chair offer is deliberately designed so that the obvious object has low prestige while the hidden power sits in scheduling authority.

# Chapter 6 — GOVERNMENT AT DAWN

Purpose:
- resolve the first major strategic arc,
- force timing decisions under market pressure,
- define the institutional architecture of the future NSO,
- form an actual government from the routes the player helped make viable.

## Government outcomes

### Reform Accord
Renewal Alliance + Civic Labour + independent support.

Strength:
reform capability combined with labour legitimacy.

Risk:
narrow parliamentary margin and dependence on pivotal votes.

### Reconstruction Coalition
Renewal Alliance + Stability Union + independents.

Strength:
administrative depth and broader implementation capacity.

Risk:
Harbor-era legitimacy burden and old-network capture risk.

### Civic Compact
Civic Labour + Stability Union + Free Cities + independent support.

Strength:
plural oversight and legitimacy architecture.

Risk:
multiple veto centers and implementation friction.

The final choice set is state-dependent. Routes that the player weakened may become unavailable.
