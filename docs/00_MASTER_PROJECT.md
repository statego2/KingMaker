# KINGMAKER — Master Project File
## Working title: *KINGMAKER: The Game of Judgment*

**Project state:** Sessions 0–4 defined  
**Primary format:** Mobile-first portrait browser strategic card RPG / learning game  
**Curriculum sources:** Baltasar Gracián, *The Art of Worldly Wisdom* (user-provided categorized text) + Robert Greene, *The 48 Laws of Power* (framework-level synthesis)  
**Core design principle:** The player should live through realistic strategic situations first; explicit principles belong mainly in optional debriefs, not as obvious “correct law” prompts.

---

# 0. Product Definition

A persistent strategic world in which the player must:
- understand incomplete and distorted information,
- govern emotion and ego,
- make decisions under uncertainty,
- time and execute actions,
- understand people and incentives,
- persuade and build voluntary alignment,
- map formal and informal power,
- manage reputation and positioning,
- reason adversarially,
- improve long-term capability and legacy.

The game should feel like a real story, not a collection of unrelated quiz cards.

## Core loop

PERCEIVE → SELF-COMMAND → FRAME → CHOOSE → ACT → OBSERVE REACTIONS → UPDATE BELIEFS → ADAPT

## Three layers of truth

1. **Objective reality** — what is actually true.
2. **Actor belief** — what each NPC believes is true.
3. **Player belief** — what the player currently thinks is true.

## Outcome principle

**Decision quality and outcome are scored separately.**  
A sound decision can produce a bad outcome; a poor decision can occasionally get lucky.

# 1. Gracián Extraction

The user-provided edition organizes the 300 maxims into six domains:

1. Perception, Judgment & Knowledge
2. Character & Self-Government
3. Action, Timing & Effectiveness
4. People, Relationships & Influence
5. Reputation, Status & Social Presence
6. Cultivation, Excellence & Development

The extraction reduced the 300 maxims to roughly 58 reusable trainable mechanisms/competencies and highlighted recurring tensions rather than treating all 300 maxims as isolated commandments.

Key Gracián lesson for the game:
**Strategic intelligence should be governed by character, judgment, and self-command rather than reduced to manipulation tricks.**

5. Consistency ↔ Adaptability
6. Visibility ↔ Scarcity

12. Principle ↔ Pragmatism

These tensions are the main source of high-difficulty questions. The advanced learner should not merely know principles; they should know when one principle should dominate another.

# 5. Mastery Scale

- **Level 0 — Blind:** does not notice the issue.
- **Level 1 — Recognition:** recognizes it when prompted.
- **Level 2 — Diagnosis:** independently identifies the real problem.
- **Level 3 — Application:** applies the principle in a clear case.
- **Level 4 — Trade-off:** weighs it against competing principles.
- **Level 5 — Adversarial:** anticipates another strategist’s response.
- **Level 6 — Adaptive Mastery:** knows when to apply, modify, or violate the principle.

# 7. Question Archetypes

## Recognition
Goal: notice the principle/risk without being asked to solve the whole case.

Possible tasks:
- Identify the hidden risk.
- Identify the distorted signal.
- Identify which relationship variable is being mistaken for another.
- Identify which competency is missing.

## Diagnosis
Goal: determine what is actually happening.

Possible tasks:
- Identify the true bottleneck.
- Rank competing explanations.
- Identify source bias.
- Determine the most likely actor motive.
- Identify the false framing.

## Application
Goal: choose the best next move.

Possible tasks:
- Gather information.
- Choose communication strategy.
- Allocate resources.
- Set boundaries.
- Reframe or delay.
- Commit or preserve optionality.

## Trade-off
Goal: choose which legitimate principle should dominate under current constraints.

Typical tensions:
- speed vs verification,
- secrecy vs trust,
- concentration vs resilience,
- loyalty vs independence,
- visibility vs scarcity.

## Adversarial
Goal: reason about an opponent who is also reasoning.

Possible tasks:
- predict opponent response,
- detect bait,
- infer second-order intent,
- choose a move robust to countermoves,
- update after strategic deception.

## Integrated
Goal: simulate professional-level case reasoning.

Required ingredients:
- 4–8 actors,
- incomplete information,
- credible competing interpretations,
- time/resource constraints,
- at least one hidden dependency,
- at least one second-order consequence,
- delayed outcome,
- no cartoonishly bad answer.

# 9. Distractor Quality Standard

A distractor is acceptable only if:
- it is locally rational,
- it represents a real principle or incentive,
- it fails because of context rather than stupidity,
- it has similar length and sophistication to the best answer,
- it does not reveal itself through wording,
- a smart learner can explain why it is tempting.

Avoid:
- joke answers,
- obvious moral villains,
- keyword matching,
- “always/never” giveaways,
- one answer much longer or more nuanced than the rest,
- repeated pattern such as option C being disproportionately correct.

# 10. Scoring

## Workbook score

Do not use only binary right/wrong for advanced questions.

Recommended internal score:
- **0.00** — clearly poor decision
- **0.25** — understands one relevant factor but misses the core issue
- **0.50** — defensible but materially weaker
- **0.75** — strong alternative
- **1.00** — best-supported answer under stated conditions

## Reasoning dimensions

For written/debrief scoring:
- Diagnosis quality
- Evidence use
- Trade-off awareness
- Second-order reasoning
- Calibration
- Ethical/long-term integrity where relevant

## Game score

Keep separate:
- **Decision Quality**
- **Outcome**
- **Reputation Effect**
- **Relationship Effect**
- **Resource Effect**
- **Long-Term Position Effect**

Never teach outcome bias by treating lucky results as proof of good reasoning.

# 11. Anti-Repetition Rules

- No more than 2 consecutive questions with the same primary competency.
- No repeated narrative shell within the same 25-question block.
- No identical master tension more than twice within 10 questions.
- Rotate actor roles: superior, subordinate, peer, rival, ally, client, public, institution.
- Rotate contexts: corporate, political, diplomatic, nonprofit, media, leadership, negotiation, project, crisis.
- Do not reuse names within independent workbook cases unless the case is intentionally serialized.
- Every 50-question block must contain at least 7 of the 10 domains.
- Every 100-question block must touch all 10 domains.
- Advanced questions must increasingly combine competencies rather than merely increase prose length.

---

# 12. Quality Gates

A question is rejected if:
- the answer is obvious from moral tone,
- the story contains irrelevant decoration without strategic function,
- the correct answer depends on information not given,
- multiple answers are equally strong but the key pretends otherwise,
- the distractors are foolish,
- the explanation merely repeats the option,
- the scenario tests trivia about a maxim instead of judgment,
- the scenario rewards manipulation when simple cooperation better fits the objective,
- the question confuses “effective” with “ethical” without explicitly defining the objective.

# Narrative Integration Contract

The project is **one coherent story game**, not a quiz collection with decorative fiction.

The 1,000-question workbook is the **curriculum and scenario laboratory**. It will feed the final game through three layers:

1. **Main Campaign — one persistent story**
   - one coherent world,
   - recurring factions and institutions,
   - recurring NPCs with memory, incentives, beliefs and relationships,
   - long causal chains,
   - consequences that can reappear many chapters later,
   - all 60 competencies and 15 master tensions represented across the campaign.

2. **Strategic Debrief / Codex**
   - after important scenes, the player may optionally inspect which competencies were involved,
   - relevant Gracián/Greene anchors are shown after the decision rather than before it,
   - decision quality is separated from outcome quality.

3. **Challenge / Workbook Mode**
   - all 1,000 calibrated questions remain playable as a separate training mode,
   - story cases can link to related workbook drills,
   - mastery data can influence recommendations without forcing the player out of the story.

## Conversion rule

The final story will **not** paste 1,000 independent workbook situations into a campaign.

Instead:

`Question structure → strategic mechanism → recurring character/world situation → story event → delayed consequence`

Example:

A workbook case about unreliable information may become a rumour involving an already-known minister, investor or adviser.  
The strategic structure remains the same, but the event is rewritten to fit the existing world, relationships and history.

## Coverage requirement for the campaign

The main campaign must:
- cover all 60 competencies,
- use all 15 master tensions,
- include recurring applications at increasing mastery levels,
- revisit earlier lessons under new conditions,
- include situations where an earlier successful heuristic later fails,
- preserve continuity of facts, relationships and consequences.

Thus the workbook teaches the underlying strategic grammar; the campaign makes the player live it.
