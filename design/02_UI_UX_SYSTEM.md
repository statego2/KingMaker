# KINGMAKER — UI / UX System

## 1. Product architecture

The campaign should revolve around six primary surfaces.

1. Briefing
2. Dossier
3. Network
4. Situation Map
5. Decision Room
6. Archive

These surfaces are the game's language.

---

## 2. Briefing

Purpose:
turn raw events into a structured but incomplete picture.

Contains:
- date and time,
- current objective,
- three to seven meaningful developments,
- source provenance,
- pressure indicators,
- unresolved threads,
- incoming messages.

Design rule:
A briefing is not a feed.

It should feel curated by circumstance and access.

Important:
different routes can produce different briefings because access differs.

---

## 3. Dossier

Purpose:
give persistent memory for people, institutions, projects and crises.

Character dossier sections:
- public role,
- known priorities,
- observed behavior,
- promises / obligations,
- relationship dimensions,
- recent changes,
- unverified beliefs,
- history with player.

Never show hidden developer truth.

Use uncertainty labels:
Confirmed
Strongly supported
Plausible
Contested
Unknown

This becomes one of the game's most important learning instruments.

---

## 4. Network

Purpose:
show relational power.

Nodes:
- people,
- institutions,
- factions,
- strategic projects.

Edge types:
- formal authority,
- trust,
- access,
- dependency,
- financial exposure,
- political cooperation,
- rivalry,
- information flow.

Do not show all edge types simultaneously.

The player chooses an analytical lens.

Example lenses:
Influence
Dependency
Coalition
Information
Implementation
Risk

This prevents visual spaghetti and teaches perspective switching.

---

## 5. Situation Map

Purpose:
show geography only when geography changes incentives or consequences.

Map layers:
- Aster Gate infrastructure,
- energy,
- ports,
- industrial capacity,
- electoral / regional pressure,
- maritime dispute,
- trade route,
- external influence,
- crisis impact.

The map should feel strategic, not military by default.

Spatial information exists to answer:
where is dependency?
where is vulnerability?
where is capacity?
where will this decision land?

---

## 6. Decision Room

This is the signature screen.

Structure:

### Header
Decision title.
Clock / timing.
Decision owner.
Irreversibility marker.

### Situation
Three to five facts.
One to three uncertainties.
One current objective.

### Stakeholder strip
Most relevant actors with:
- current stance,
- confidence of inference,
- what they control.

### Option cards
Usually 3–5.

Each option should expose:
- action,
- immediate cost,
- commitment level,
- visible risk,
- timing.

Do not show the game's judgment of the option before choice.

### Optional analysis tray
Player can inspect:
- network view,
- past promises,
- source trail,
- resource impact.

Opening analysis costs attention or time only when the narrative supports that pressure.

### Commit interaction
A deliberate hold or slide can be used for genuinely irreversible decisions.
Do not overuse.

---

## 7. Consequence presentation

Never reduce major outcomes to:
+5 Trust
-3 Reputation

Instead:

Immediate consequence panel:
- what happened,
- who reacted,
- what became newly known,
- what changed structurally.

Then allow a subtle analytical layer:
Trust with Lea: strengthened
Meridian exposure: increased
Optionality: reduced
Public narrative: unstable

Numbers can exist behind expandable detail.

---

## 8. Archive

Purpose:
make the campaign feel historical.

Stores:
- major decisions,
- promises,
- broken commitments,
- public statements,
- institutions created,
- crises survived,
- casualties or irreversible costs,
- changes in law,
- key intelligence.

At Act VI, the Archive becomes evidence.

This is essential to the legacy theme.

## 10. Choice presentation rules

Choices must not be encoded as:
good / neutral / evil.

Use strategically distinct verbs.

Example:
Delay
Verify
Trade
Delegate
Escalate
Reframe
Commit
Exit
Expose
Absorb
Split
Concede
Insulate
Sequence

Good options often differ by:
- time horizon,
- reversibility,
- who bears cost,
- who gains control,
- information required,
- legitimacy.

---

## 11. Mobile interaction principles

One-hand usability matters.

Primary action zone should remain reachable with thumb.

Avoid:
- tiny graph hit targets,
- horizontal text carousels,
- persistent bottom sheets that cover content,
- swipe gestures with hidden meaning.

Use:
- tap for inspect,
- press-and-hold for provenance or history,
- drag only when spatial manipulation is the mechanic,
- explicit close / back behavior.

---

## 12. Cognitive load

The player is here to think strategically, not decode interface chrome.

At any moment:
- one primary question,
- maximum three secondary signals,
- deeper information available on demand.

The game can be intellectually hard while the interface remains clear.

---

## 13. Accessibility

Minimum:
- WCAG AA contrast targets,
- color-independent semantic states,
- scalable text,
- motion reduction,
- haptic reduction,
- dyslexia-friendly spacing mode if feasible,
- all charts readable without color alone.

Serious strategy benefits from readability.
