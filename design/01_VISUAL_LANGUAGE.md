# KINGMAKER — Visual Language

## 1. Art direction name

STATECRAFT NOIR

A restrained near-future political aesthetic combining institutional materiality, editorial precision and strategic information systems.

Not cyberpunk.
Not retro cold-war cosplay.
Not generic dark mode.

The world is 2032: recognizable, credible, slightly more integrated and data-rich than today.

---

## 2. Visual contrast model

KINGMAKER lives between two materials:

### Institutional material
- stone,
- paper,
- stamped documents,
- brass,
- linen,
- official seals,
- archival photography,
- parliamentary architecture.

### Strategic system material
- glass,
- map layers,
- graph nodes,
- live timelines,
- confidence bands,
- operational overlays,
- restrained data visualization.

The visual identity should repeatedly place these two worlds beside each other.

That contrast expresses the core theme:
old institutions operating through new systems under permanent human ambiguity.

---

## 3. Color system

### Core neutrals

Obsidian — #0B0E13
Primary deep background. Never pure black.

Graphite — #141A22
Primary panel surface.

Slate — #202833
Raised interactive surface.

Fog — #98A2AF
Secondary information.

Paper — #E7E1D6
Primary light text and document field.

Bone — #F3EEE4
High-contrast reading surface.

### Identity accent

Brass — #B4935B
Use for authority, section focus, important institutional accents and the KINGMAKER mark.

Brass should never become a reward color sprayed everywhere.

### Semantic accents

Signal Teal — #4F8D89
Verified / stable / constructive system condition.

Crisis Amber — #C8953D
Pressure, narrowing time, material caution.

Oxide Red — #8E4B46
Escalation, grievance, danger, irreversible harm.

Indigo — #5F678F
Intelligence, ambiguity, external systems, classified analytical layer.

Ash Green — #6F806E
Resilience, reserve, institutional capacity.

### Principle

Color communicates category or state.
Color does not communicate emotional manipulation.

---

## 4. Typography

### Display

A high-contrast editorial serif.

Desired traits:
- political gravitas without historical costume,
- sharp capitals,
- strong numerals,
- elegant chapter titles,
- excellent mobile rendering.

Uses:
- act cards,
- chapter titles,
- major quotations,
- ending families,
- institutional names.

### Interface

A highly readable humanist or neo-grotesk sans.

Desired traits:
- compact but calm,
- tabular numbers,
- strong hierarchy at 12–18px,
- excellent Greek and Latin support if localization expands.

Uses:
- choices,
- dossier labels,
- data,
- map annotations,
- relationship values,
- briefings.

### Monospace

Use sparingly for:
- timestamps,
- source IDs,
- system logs,
- classified provenance,
- coordinates,
- procedural references.

Do not let monospace dominate the product.

## 5. Layout grammar

### Default composition

Portrait mobile screen:
- 8–12% top context
- 55–70% primary content
- 15–25% decision / action zone
- persistent bottom navigation only when the scene allows it

### Visual depth

Maximum three simultaneous depth planes:
1. background world layer,
2. primary content surface,
3. transient focus surface.

Avoid endless nested cards.

### Corners

Mostly 8–14 px equivalent radius.
Institutional documents can use square or near-square corners.
Soft rounded consumer-app cards should be rare.

### Dividers

Prefer:
- fine rules,
- spacing,
- alignment,
- section titles.

Avoid boxing every information group.

---

## 6. Texture and material

Use texture at 2–6% perceptual intensity.

Allowed:
- paper grain,
- brushed dark metal,
- map fibers,
- archival scan noise,
- extremely subtle glass bloom.

Avoid:
- heavy grunge,
- fake scratches,
- cinematic dirt,
- tactical military clichés.

Texture should make the product tactile, not dirty.

---

## 7. Photography and portrait language

Character portraits should feel like editorial intelligence photography.

### Framing
- chest-up or shoulder-up,
- neutral or institutional environment,
- controlled negative space,
- eye-line useful for composition,
- no superhero angles.

### Lighting
- directional naturalistic light,
- restrained contrast,
- occasional window or office practicals,
- no neon rim-light.

### Wardrobe
- credible contemporary political / professional wardrobe,
- faction identity through subtle material, cut and setting,
- not uniform color coding.

### Expression
Neutral is not enough.

Each portrait should communicate:
- public mask,
- current pressure,
- disposition.

Avoid exaggerated villain smiles or obvious archetypes.

---

## 8. Institutional identity

Each major institution gets:

- a restrained seal or wordmark,
- one dominant geometric motif,
- one texture,
- one secondary accent,
- a document header system.

Examples:

Presidency:
vertical symmetry, brass line, pale stone.

National Strategy Office:
concentric node motif, slate, precision grid.

Assembly:
radial chamber geometry, paper, dark red secondary accent.

Aureon:
industrial arc, graphite + warm metal, controlled corporate polish.

Nexa:
modular lattice, cooler grey + indigo, technical minimalism.

Civic Wire:
editorial black/white, hard rules, evidence-first.

Ordan diplomatic material:
formal, elegant, slightly colder, no "evil empire" coding.

---

## 9. Faction identity rules

Political factions should be recognizable but not sports teams.

Use:
- typographic mark,
- geometric motif,
- subtle color family,
- rally / media photography style,
- document system.

Do not turn every scene into five colored badges.

Faction color appears when affiliation is analytically relevant.

---

## 10. Data visualization style

Charts should look like analytical instruments.

Use:
- thin axes,
- clear labels,
- confidence bands,
- historical markers,
- small multiples where necessary,
- direct annotation.

Avoid:
- 3D charts,
- glossy gradients,
- needless animation,
- giant donuts,
- meaningless dashboard decoration.

---

## 11. Motion language

Motion should imply:
- arrival,
- reclassification,
- connection,
- pressure,
- consequence.

Examples:
- a new dossier slides into an inbox stack,
- a relationship edge redraws after a revelation,
- a chapter history marker resolves into place,
- a deadline ring contracts,
- one option visually disappears after a commitment.

Timing:
- 120–180 ms for interface state
- 220–420 ms for analytical transformations
- 600–1200 ms for chapter / act transitions

Use long transitions only when the narrative deserves ceremony.

---

## 12. Visual progression by act

Act I:
more paper, isolated cards, narrow field of view.

Act II:
hybrid paper + system interface, first maps.

Act III:
network and coalition surfaces become central.

Act IV:
provenance, contradiction, source confidence and adversarial overlays.

Act V:
system map, crisis stack, compressed information, priority hierarchy.

Act VI:
archives, long-horizon diagrams, succession graph, visual calm.

The player should be able to identify the act from a screenshot without reading the title.
