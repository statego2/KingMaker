# KINGMAKER — Game Feel and Pacing

## 1. Core interaction rhythm

The rhythm should repeatedly alternate:

Observe → Investigate → Interpret → Commit → Wait → Reinterpret

The key word is wait.

Not every consequence should arrive immediately.

Delayed consequence is part of the game feel.

---

## 2. Session rhythm

A good 15–25 minute mobile session can contain:

1. one briefing,
2. two to four short interactions,
3. one meaningful decision,
4. one consequence or reveal,
5. one unresolved hook.

Longer core chapters can expand this pattern without losing it.

---

## 3. Tension curve

Do not keep every scene intense.

Use three pressure states.

### Open
Time available.
Research allowed.
Player explores.

### Closing
A deadline or political window is narrowing.
Some options may disappear.

### Locked
A commitment has been made or external events force response.

The interface can reflect these states through density, clock treatment and available actions.

---

## 4. Information reveal

Revelations should alter models, not merely add lore.

Strong reveal:
"The donor was connected to Aureon" is weak by itself.

Better:
"The donor funded two opposing factions through different intermediaries, which means your assumption that funding equals command is unreliable."

A reveal should ideally change:
- actor model,
- causal model,
- option value,
- confidence.

---

## 5. Decision weight

Use three classes.

### Tactical decision
Consequence horizon: scene to chapter.
Commitment: low.
Frequency: common.

### Strategic decision
Consequence horizon: several chapters or an act.
Commitment: medium-high.
Frequency: limited.

### Constitutional decision
Consequence horizon: remainder of campaign / legacy.
Commitment: high or irreversible.
Frequency: rare.

The visual ceremony should scale with class.

---

## 6. Feedback philosophy

The game should usually tell the player what happened before telling them what it means.

Step 1:
observable outcome.

Step 2:
actor reactions.

Step 3:
state changes.

Step 4:
optional debrief.

This preserves discovery.

---

## 7. Success language

Avoid:
PERFECT!
GREAT CHOICE!

Prefer:
The coalition held.
The financing window remains open.
Lea accepted the constraint, but not the framing.
Your recommendation is now part of the official record.

The world reacts; the game does not cheerlead.

---

## 8. Failure language

Avoid:
WRONG.
MISSION FAILED.

Prefer:
The vote passed, but implementation authority moved to a ministry you do not control.
Your source was accurate about the document and wrong about the motive.
The crisis stabilized. Meridian is now dependent on emergency liquidity.

Strategic failure should produce learning, not humiliation.

---

## 9. Haptics

Use sparingly.

Suggested:
- light confirmation: opening a secured dossier
- medium: irreversible commitment
- sharp short pulse: deadline state changes
- double subtle pulse: contradictory intelligence arrives

Never haptic every tap.

---

## 10. Sound interaction direction

Not soundtrack; interface sound language.

Use:
- soft paper movement,
- restrained seal / stamp,
- quiet glass or metal click,
- distant room tone,
- secure message arrival,
- map layer activation.

Avoid:
- sci-fi bleeps,
- casino chimes,
- military radio cliché,
- loud success stingers.

---

## 11. Narrative compression

Mobile scenes should not dump every argument in dialogue.

Use a three-layer model:

Visible:
what the player must know now.

Expandable:
context, history, evidence.

Archival:
full timeline, prior commitments, long notes.

This supports intellectual depth without killing pace.

---

## 12. The "one more scene" engine

End sessions on one of four hooks:

- new information that reclassifies a person,
- a deadline that makes tomorrow different,
- a request from an important actor,
- a consequence from an old choice.

Do not rely on cheap cliffhangers.

The hook should emerge from system continuity.
