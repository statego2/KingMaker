# KINGMAKER — Creative Direction

## 1. North Star

KINGMAKER is a game about learning to perceive systems of power before trying to control them.

The visual and interaction design must therefore communicate three things simultaneously:

1. incomplete information,
2. interconnected consequences,
3. increasing responsibility.

The player should begin as someone receiving fragments and end as someone responsible for the architecture that produces those fragments.

That transformation should be visible in the interface itself.

---

## 2. Player fantasy

The fantasy is not "I am the ruler."

It is:

I understand what others miss.
I can read the room beneath the room.
My advice changes the path of institutions.
People remember what I did.
My past choices alter what is possible now.
Eventually I must decide whether the system can survive without me.

That is more interesting than a power fantasy because the player's influence grows faster than certainty.

---

## 3. Six-act experiential arc

### Act I — The Outsider
Emotion: curiosity, ambiguity, humility.

Visual state:
- narrow information windows,
- incomplete dossiers,
- low access,
- muted interface,
- more paper and received documents than live controls,
- relationship network mostly hidden.

The player should feel intelligent but peripheral.

### Act II — The Operator
Emotion: traction, responsibility, momentum.

Visual state:
- first real dashboard surfaces,
- project timelines,
- conditional access to ministries and actors,
- visible state variables,
- clearer network edges,
- higher information density without feeling omniscient.

The player gains instruments, not certainty.

### Act III — The Kingmaker
Emotion: leverage, temptation, consequence.

Visual state:
- multiple live coalitions,
- agenda sequencing,
- reputation and dependency surfaces become visually important,
- the map of power becomes denser,
- briefings begin to contain contradictions from actors who all matter.

This should be the first act where the UI feels "powerful."

### Act IV — The Counterplayer
Emotion: suspicion, adaptation, self-awareness.

Visual state:
- conflicting intelligence layers,
- source confidence,
- provenance trails,
- signs that other actors are modeling the player,
- controlled visual interference and ambiguity,
- relationship graph edges can be intentionally misleading until verified.

The game should visually remind the player that information is contested.

### Act V — The System
Emotion: pressure, triage, coordination.

Visual state:
- multiple concurrent crisis channels,
- clock pressure,
- system health,
- risk propagation,
- resource allocation,
- institutional capacity.

This is not visual chaos. It is disciplined compression under stress.

### Act VI — Legacy
Emotion: reflection, restraint, permanence.

Visual state:
- reduced noise,
- long-horizon graphs,
- succession map,
- institutional durability,
- personal centrality versus system resilience,
- archived decisions resurfacing as evidence of what the player built.

The final act should feel visually calmer even when the stakes are greatest.

## 4. Design pillars

### Pillar A — Information has provenance

No important fact should feel like floating game text.

Every claim should visually belong to a source:
- official memo,
- journalist,
- market data,
- private message,
- intelligence note,
- personal observation,
- inference.

The player learns to distinguish fact from interpretation by visual grammar, not only prose.

### Pillar B — Power is relational

Avoid giant "POWER 73" meters as the primary representation.

Power should be shown through:
- access,
- dependency,
- control over timing,
- resources,
- coalition structure,
- gatekeeping,
- reputation,
- institutional authority.

Use numbers internally; express structure visually.

### Pillar C — Consequences leave residue

A choice should leave traces:
- annotations in a dossier,
- a relationship edge changes tone or thickness,
- a promise appears in an obligations ledger,
- a chapter receives a historical marker,
- a later briefing cites an old decision.

The world should look accumulated.

### Pillar D — Serious, not sterile

Premium political design often collapses into grey dashboards.

KINGMAKER needs atmosphere:
- beautiful typography,
- restrained material textures,
- editorial composition,
- subtle map motion,
- layered light,
- real visual identity for institutions and factions.

### Pillar E — Tension without casino tricks

Avoid:
- flashing rewards,
- aggressive confetti,
- fake urgency,
- slot-machine motion,
- glowing loot rarity logic.

Use:
- silence,
- countdowns,
- document arrival,
- changing access,
- subtle motion,
- compressed choices,
- visual loss of optionality.

### Pillar F — The player earns visual authority

Do not reveal the "full dashboard" at the beginning.

UI complexity itself is progression.

Unlocking a new analytical surface should feel like gaining institutional access.

---

## 5. Emotional targets

A strong KINGMAKER session should produce some combination of:

- "I think I understand what is actually going on."
- "I am not sure this source is telling me the whole truth."
- "That person matters more than their title suggests."
- "I can see why the obvious move is dangerous."
- "I remember promising them something."
- "I caused this three chapters ago."
- "I won the vote but made the system worse."
- "I have become powerful enough that my own habits are now a vulnerability."
- "What I build matters more than whether I personally remain central."

---

## 6. Tone boundaries

The world is serious but not nihilistic.

Do not visually imply that:
- everyone is secretly corrupt,
- institutions are fake,
- manipulation is always superior,
- cruelty equals intelligence,
- cynicism equals realism.

The art direction should support ambiguity, not hopelessness.

---

## 7. Premium reference qualities

Not direct visual copying, but target qualities:

- prestige political thriller: controlled tension and intelligence
- financial terminal: information density with hierarchy
- intelligence dossier: provenance, uncertainty and annotation
- premium editorial magazine: typography and whitespace
- operations room: system overview and prioritization
- grand strategy map: spatial consequences without becoming war-game clutter

The final synthesis should be unmistakably KINGMAKER rather than a collage of references.

---

## 8. The one-sentence test

Before approving any screen, ask:

Does this make the player feel like a strategist working inside a living state, or like a person using an app?

If the answer is "an app," redesign it.
